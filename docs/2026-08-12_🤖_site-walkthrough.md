# A walk through the Neotone site

### Greeting

Hello, sziasztok, from the Neotone workshop in Budapest.

We're going to walk through the wireframe together, in the order somebody moves through it. By the end you'll have seen every kind of page we have, and you'll be able to place any new page we're asked to build.

---

### The menu

*[Open the site.]*

Three things across the top: **Neotone**, **One**, **neOS**. A company, an instrument, a piece of software.

We can afford a menu like this because we make one instrument. Ableton has five product lines and several hundred packs, so every slot in their menu has to be a product, and their writing ends up in a blog behind a More button. Teenage Engineering has forty products, same situation. With one instrument, our slots are free, and we spend them on what a reader wants instead of on what we sell.

This holds as we grow. Anima and Treangle will be expressions of the same values as the One, so three instruments would be three expressions of one position.

---

### The home page

We're on it. Three pieces, each with an image, a small kicker, a title, the opening lines, and a link into the piece.

This is a **container**: a page that holds articles. It's the first of our page types.

What it holds is the culture side of belonging. Somebody here is asking what they'd be part of, and we answer with people, ideas and materials.

*[Scroll.]*

Each piece gets close to a screen to itself, so you arrive at one rather than scanning a list.

The running order is ours to choose. A new piece can go anywhere in it, and somebody makes that call every time we publish.

Which makes the lead piece the most important decision on the site. It carries the weight a statement of what Neotone is would carry, so it has to be something a stranger can hold: a person, an object, a place. Somebody landing cold on a photograph of a player knows within a second that this isn't a gear site, and that's what buys us their next five minutes. A piece of pure argument in that position leaves them nothing to take hold of.

### A piece

*[Click into the first one.]*

This is an **article**, the second type.

It has its own address, which is what lets search find it, lets you send it to somebody, and lets a Tonefield issue collect it later.

It's written to hold up whenever somebody reads it, so nothing in it depends on when it went up. That's the difference from a post, which belongs to the week it was published, and it's why we show no dates.

*[Scroll down.]*

The image runs wide and the text sits in a narrower column. At the bottom, **Read next**, with one piece chosen because it follows from this one.

*[Look at the top left.]*

As we scrolled, the Neotone mark shrank into the corner. That's the way back.

*[Click it.]*

We scroll up, the mark grows again, and we land in the feed at the place we left it.

That's the whole navigation system: **every page returns to its parent, and the shrinking mark is how.** An article goes back to the container that holds it. Anything without a container goes to the home page. There's no separate trail of links across the top and nothing at the foot of the piece, because the mark is in view the whole way down.

### The end of the feed

*[Scroll to the bottom.]*

Past the last piece, **Load more**, and under it a mailing list sign-up.

Load more continues the reading. The sign-up is for somebody who's decided to stop, which is why it comes second. Once the feed runs out, the button goes and the sign-up becomes the end of the page.

*[Keep scrolling.]*

The footer. The three destinations again, a search field, and a row of links: Workshop, Manual, Contact, Legal. Then where we are, and where else we are.

Nobody arrives here by browsing. You get here by searching or by needing something specific, which is why it can hold unrelated things and stay useful.

*[Click Neotone in the footer.]*

It scrolls to the top before the site changes, so the mark grows back first.

### The manual

*[Click Manual in the footer.]*

Third type, a **utility page**. It's built exactly like the article we just read, and the difference is the mark: the plain Neotone one, and it returns to the home page, because a utility page has no container.

This is where somebody goes with a question. It tells you which control does what, and every section has its own address, so a tooltip inside the software can link straight to the part that answers the question in front of a player.

*[Open a section.]*

A section is written in three passes: the basic walkthrough, then a layer for deeper functionality, then the questions underneath. The first two are manual writing, which is to say practical. You have the instrument in front of you and you want to do a thing.

*[Point at the third group.]*

This last group is the important part of this page. Each item there carries a **neOS** marker, and following one takes you out of the manual.

That's on purpose. Explaining the instrument three times at increasing depth is how its reasoning gets written down in the first place. The third pass is where we find out what we actually think, and that material has somewhere to go.

### neOS

Here's the second container. Feed of pieces, load more, sign-up, footer.

*[Look above the first piece.]*

This page carries one line, telling you what neOS is. We need it here because neOS is a word we made up, and a reader arriving cold has no way into it.

This container holds the craft side of belonging. Somebody here has found depth they can take part in. So the two containers are two halves of one feeling: on the home page, *I want to be part of this*. Here, *this has something to teach me*.

And that tells us which manual material gets to become a piece. **It travels when it holds for somebody who will never touch the control it describes.** Why a scale spells a note the way it does is interesting to a reader who never opens the scale builder. A list of MIDI messages isn't, so that stays in the manual.

### One

*[Click One.]*

We've come here last, and plenty of people will come here first. It sits in the middle of the menu, and it's the page a search result or a shared link lands on, so somebody can easily arrive with none of the context we just walked through.

That's fine, and the page is built for it. It doesn't lean on anything we've seen. What it does carry is a way back out to the context, which we'll get to in a moment.

This is the fourth type, a **product page**. It sits at the same level as a container rather than inside one.

It opens with a line and a short orientation, then the specifications, then the choice between an instrument built to order and one from stock, then accessories. Somebody arriving here has already decided, so the page orients them and gets out of the way. The place we slow a reader down is the home page.

Prices include tax for wherever you say you are, and that choice follows you around the site.

### Keeping somebody on this page

Two things on this page do the same job, and it's worth seeing them as one idea.

*[Add something, and open the selection panel.]*

**Selection** is a panel rather than a page. It holds what you've chosen so far, because you can pick more than one thing, and it opens over the page instead of navigating away from it. Leaving would mean abandoning a configuration in progress.

*[Close it, scroll to the articles below the purchase flow.]*

These are pieces about the wait, the material, and the workshop, and this is the way back out. Somebody who arrived here first, knowing nothing about us, picks up the context from these rather than from having walked the site in order.

They open in a new tab, for the same reason as the panel: their home is the Neotone container, so the mark at the top of them would carry somebody mid-purchase back into culture.

A panel and a new tab, both keeping this page intact behind whatever you're looking at.

*[Scroll on.]*

Then what happens next, in four steps, the purchase terms, and an invitation to come and play one first.

### Checkout

*[Open the selection panel and continue to checkout.]*

Checkout is the second half of this product page. It carries the One mark and returns to One, so it's the same species living at a second address, rather than a utility page.

Here the arithmetic is visible: what each item costs before tax, any discount, then the tax, then the total. Everywhere else on the site a price is just a price.

---

### The four types

We've seen all of them:

1. **Container.** Holds articles. Neotone and neOS.
2. **Article.** An individual piece with its own address.
3. **Utility.** Footer material. Built like an article, carries the plain Neotone mark, returns to the home page.
4. **Product page.** Carries an instrument and its purchase, and reaches to checkout. Sits at the same level as a container.

When somebody asks for a new page, that's the question: **which of the four is it?** If it collects one thing, it's an article. If it collects several, it's a container, and a container earns a menu slot only when one type has outgrown it. Three instruments are three menu items.

Selection is a component, like the specifications strip. Components live on pages, and they don't need a fifth category.

---

### One last thing about the pieces themselves

The workshop article you saw below the purchase flow is the same article as Workshop in the footer. One address, two ways in.

That's how we want everything to work. A piece sits in its container, gets linked from the product page at the moment it's relevant, and can be collected into a Tonefield issue later. Anything written only to hold a page together does one job, and that's the most expensive kind of writing to keep.

That's the walk.
