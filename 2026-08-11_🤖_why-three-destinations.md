# Why this site has three destinations

Neotone, One, neOS. 

Not Products, Blog, Videos, Artists, Features, Press or Resources.

This is not minimalism as taste. It is the structure that follows from what Neotone actually is.

---

## Structure follows constraints

A site with Products, Features, Blog, Videos, Artists and Docs is not badly designed. It is designed for several product lines, several buyer segments, and a team large enough to feed every section. Each section is an entrance for a different visitor, which is why such sites duplicate: the same artist appears on the artists page, in a video, in a blog post and in a feature. Duplication is a feature of that model, not a flaw in it.

Neotone has one product line, one workshop, one voice, and about one piece of writing a week. Six sections fed at that rate update each one every six weeks, which advertises the company's size in the least flattering way available.

Two further costs, both about the writing rather than the layout. A blog is reverse-chronological and undifferentiated, which is correct for a lot of writing of variable quality and wrong for a little writing of high quality: it hides your best work behind whatever came last. And a blog has to be fed. A cadence lowers the bar, because the alternative to publishing something mediocre is a page that looks abandoned. Curated containers carry no cadence, so every piece can be good.

So:
Buyer segments, volume and quality of content.

---

## What we do instead

| Destination | What the reader wants | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| **Neotone** | Belonging | A way of making music you recognise as yours, and the people already doing it |
| **One** | The instrument | The object, and the purchase |
| **neOS** | Craft Knowledge | The intelligence inside the instrument, made visible |

Belonging and craft are **things a reader wants to become, not categories of content.** A piece rarely sits ambiguously between them.

Neotone answers *what will I be a part of.* neOS answers *how does someone who really understands this think about it.* Craft depth, not technical depth: digital lutherie belongs, firmware instructions do not.

Every piece does more than one job. An article sits in its container, is linked from the product page where it is relevant, and is eligible for a Tonefield issue. Prose written only to hold a page together does one job and is the most expensive kind to maintain.

Questions are answered where they arise: lead time in the specs, what happens next under the order, can I change my mind beside the commitment. That is what makes an FAQ unnecessary rather than merely unfashionable.

## Where the writing comes from

This is the part that makes the rest survivable, and it is the claim to defend hardest.

**The manual is the practical layer, neOS the intelligence layer.** The manual says which control and what it does, and lives in the footer and inside the software beside the control it explains. neOS says why the instrument behaves that way, and the manual links to it where the question occurs.

The manual is written in three registers: the basic walkthrough, a layer for deeper functionality, and a layer that reaches the craft and philosophical questions underneath. They live together on the page. Explaining the instrument three times at increasing depth is how its intelligence gets articulated in the first place, so writing the manual is what generates neOS material. Documentation is not overhead set against the editorial. It is the method that produces it.

neOS material comes from the first and last of those registers. An introduction has to explain the premise, why the instrument is different in kind, and that framing stands alone: how the sensors read continuous multidimensional data rather than firing MIDI events is a piece whether or not you own one. The deepest register carries the craft reasoning. The middle register, which control does what and in what order, is purely operational, and it stays in the manual.

**The promotion test** is whether a piece holds for someone who will never touch that control. Why a Kurd scale spells that note as a B flat is interesting to a reader who never opens the scale builder. A MIDI implementation chart is not. The first becomes a neOS piece, the second stays in the manual.

A company with one instrument and no content operation cannot commission its way to a publication. It can only notice that the work of explaining the instrument properly already produces one.

## How it reads

One rule, from which the rest follows: **never claim what the reader has not been shown.**

An opening exists to gloss a name the reader cannot yet decode. neOS is a coined word, One is a five-figure purchase, Anima is a product nobody has heard of, so each carries one line. Neotone is on the masthead and needs no gloss, so the home page has no opening at all. It opens on its lead piece.

That is the rule doing real work rather than saving a sentence. A page that announced "Neotone is a new musical culture" would be making the claim from outside the thing it describes. Opening instead on a handpan player from Iran, a piece about wood, a piece about a tetrachord, makes the same claim and lets the reader reach it. Nobody announces a culture from within one. They publish, and the conclusion is drawn.

It follows that the running order is editorial, not chronological. With no heading, position one is the claim, so what sits there is a decision taken every time something is published.

Three registers have been tried and rejected, and should not return: superlatives, which cannot be checked and so are not believed; coinages the reader has not yet earned; and nameless slogans, which read as product-page taglines rather than as anything written by a person. Each fails the same way. Each claims something the reader has not been shown.

## Objections

**"You need an Artists page."** Artists appear as profiles and interviews, where they are read. A grid of faces flattens people into thumbnails, and nobody scrolls past the second row.

**"You need Videos."** A video is a format, not a subject. It belongs inside the piece it illustrates, and that piece can sit in either container: a neOS piece may be prose, video, or prose built around video. Species and format are independent. Organising by file type serves the person who made the files.

**"More pages help search."** Search rewards depth and uniqueness. Thin pages compete with each other for the same terms.

**"It seems too simple."** A handmade instrument with a sixty day wait is not bought by someone comparing feature grids. It is bought by someone deciding whether these people are serious. Publishing rarely and well demonstrates that.

**"Sunhouse does it the other way."** Sunhouse sells a modular kit: sensors, cymbals, hi-hat, interface, software, sound packs. Second-level navigation and a Build Your Kit configurator exist because a buyer must choose components. Neotone One is one object with a choice of wood. Their "Stories" section is a blog with the label changed, which is the cosmetic version of this problem rather than a solution to it, and their support is spread across five places. What is absent there is the thing being attempted here: no container exposes how the instrument thinks. Their structure is a correct response to different constraints, and copying the output of different constraints is how a small company ends up looking like a large one doing it badly.

---

## The test for anything new

**What species is it?** Article, container, section page, or utility. There is no fifth thing.

**What does the reader want?** If it is belonging or craft, it is an article in an existing container. If it collects one thing, it is an article, not a container.

A container appears only when a species outgrows the nav. Three products are three nav items.
