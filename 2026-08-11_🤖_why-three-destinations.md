# Why the site is built this way

The site does two things. It lets a reader understand what they would be joining, and it sells them an instrument. Every decision below follows from those two, in that order.

Neotone makes one instrument. That fact is what allows the site to be organised around meaning instead of around a catalogue, and it is the advantage to protect.

The examples throughout are Ableton, Teenage Engineering and Sunhouse, taken from reading their sites. Each is a competent response to a constraint Neotone does not have: all three sell more products than a nav has slots, so their navigation has to be organised by product, and everything that is not a product has to go somewhere else.

Teenage Engineering is the one to be careful with, because its site is the best of the three. It persuades through the object: photography, exploded diagrams, and short display slogans in place of prose. A company whose case is written cannot take its structure from one that succeeds by saying very little.

---

## Three destinations, ordered by what the reader wants

| Destination | The reader wants | It holds |
|---|---|---|
| **Neotone** | Belonging | A way of making music you recognise as yours, and the people already doing it |
| **One** | The instrument | The object, and the purchase |
| **neOS** | Craft | The intelligence inside the instrument, made visible |

Belonging and craft are states a reader wants to reach. Both are things a person becomes, which is why a piece almost never sits ambiguously between the two.

Neotone answers *what will I be part of.* neOS answers *how does someone who really understands this think about it.* That is craft depth. Digital lutherie belongs there; firmware instructions do not.

A nav made of products cannot hold a comparison between products, because a comparison is not a product. Ableton sells Live, Push, Move, Note and Link, each with its own sub-navigation, and no page sets one against another: the Push page never mentions Move, does not say which edition of Live it needs, and does not state what software is in the box. "Compare editions" exists, and only inside Live. A visitor is inside a single product before they have any basis for choosing it, and the material that would help them choose has nowhere to live. That site works for someone renewing. It assumes you arrived knowing.

Ordering by reader state instead gives the two questions a newcomer has, what is this world and how does this thing think, a container each.

## The nav names things that exist

Neotone, One and neOS are a company, an instrument and a piece of software. A reader can point at each one.

Features, Resources, Press and Stories name the activities of a marketing department. A nav built from them describes the company's internal structure to someone who has no reason to care about it.

## Every piece has one home and does more than one job

An article lives in its container, is linked from the product page wherever it is relevant, and is eligible for a Tonefield issue. One address, several appearances.

So artists appear as profiles and interviews, and a video sits inside the piece it illustrates. Format is independent of species: a neOS piece may be prose, video, or prose built around video. Organising by file type serves the person who made the files.

Teenage Engineering publishes real long-form work, including a substantial origin story for the APC–2 and a running series of artist interviews, and links to none of it from any product page. Each piece does one job, once, and then it is archive.

Prose written only to hold a page together also does one job, and it is the most expensive kind to maintain.

## Questions are answered where they arise

Lead time sits in the specs. What happens next sits under the order. Can I change my mind sits beside the commitment. A reader is always on the page where the question occurred to them, which is where the answer belongs.

An FAQ moves those answers somewhere else and then has to be kept in step with the pages that already contain them, and it multiplies: Sunhouse answers questions across a support page, an FAQ, a downloads page, a user guide on one subdomain and a forum on another. Each is defensible alone. Together they are a situation where a person with a question does not know where to look.

## The manual produces the editorial

This is the mechanism the whole plan depends on, and it is the claim to defend hardest.

The manual is the practical layer. It says which control does what, and it lives in the footer and inside the software beside the control it explains. neOS is the intelligence layer. It says why the instrument behaves that way, and the manual links to it where the question turns into a why.

The manual is written in three registers: the basic walkthrough, a layer for deeper functionality, and a layer that reaches the craft and philosophical questions underneath. Explaining the instrument three times at increasing depth is how its intelligence gets articulated in the first place. Writing the manual is therefore what generates neOS material. Documentation is the method that produces the editorial.

Material comes from the first and last registers. An introduction has to explain the premise, and that framing stands alone: how the sensors read continuous multidimensional data instead of firing MIDI events is a piece whether or not you own one. The deepest register carries the craft reasoning. The middle register, which control does what and in what order, stays in the manual.

**The promotion test:** a piece holds for someone who will never touch the control it describes. Why a Kurd scale spells that note as a B flat interests a reader who never opens the scale builder. A MIDI implementation chart does not.

When there is no room for this material it leaves the site. Ableton's best teaching work is not on ableton.com: Learning Music, Learning Synths, Making Music and Explore Tunings are four separate subdomains reached through the help section, and Loop, the conference that did more than anything else to make Ableton an institution, now redirects to a blog category. A nav with five products in it has nowhere else to put a way of thinking.

A company with one instrument and no content operation cannot commission its way to a publication. It can notice that the work of explaining the instrument properly already produces one.

## A container is edited, not fed

The containers are not in reverse chronological order. A piece can be added without going to the top, so position one is a judgement about what should meet a reader first.

Two things separate this from a blog with a better name. One is structural and holds on its own: there are two containers split by what the reader wants, so every piece is assigned to belonging or to craft before it is published, and there is no single undifferentiated stream for things to fall into. The other is a practice, and it has to be performed every time: deciding where a new piece goes in the order.

The practice is the fragile part, and the failure is common enough to be the default. Sunhouse calls its blog Stories. Teenage Engineering calls its blog `now`, a single reverse-chronological stream where a firmware release note sits between two artist interviews, with no categories at all. Ableton keeps the word blog and folds artists, news, podcasts, tutorials and videos into it as categories, then puts it in a "More" overflow menu, so it cannot be an entrance for anyone who has not already arrived. In each case the label was chosen carefully and the behaviour was inherited without being chosen.

A Neotone container is a vertical list of previews with a load more button, which is interactively a feed, and load more implies an ordered archive. If that order quietly becomes newest first, the curation is only a label.

## Nothing claims what the reader has not been shown

An opening exists to gloss a name the reader cannot yet decode. neOS is a coined word, One is a five-figure purchase, Anima is a product nobody has heard of, so each carries one line. Neotone is on the masthead, so the home page carries nothing and opens on its lead piece.

A page announcing "Neotone is a new musical culture" makes the claim from outside the thing it describes. Opening on a handpan player from Iran, a piece about wood, a piece about a tetrachord, puts the same claim in front of the reader and lets them reach it themselves.

Three registers have been tried and rejected: superlatives, which cannot be checked; coinages the reader has not yet earned; and nameless slogans, which read as taglines.

## A container appears only when a species outgrows the nav

Three products are three nav items. A fourth destination has to earn its place by holding a kind of writing that has nowhere else to go.

## The test for anything new

**What species is it?** Article, container, section page, or utility. There is no fifth thing.

**What does the reader want?** If it is belonging or craft, it is an article in an existing container. If it collects one thing, it is an article.

**Does it help someone understand what they would be joining, or help them get an instrument?** If neither, it does not go on the site.
