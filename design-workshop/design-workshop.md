# Neotone

## Background

Neotone is a workshop of twelve people in Budapest. We make an electronic musical instrument with a wooden body and a high level of craftsmanship. Its roots are in the acoustic handpan, and its future points toward electronic music and music learning more broadly. A lot of our materials were built quickly and by necessity. We have spent the past months articulating what the company is, its values and its voice, and we are now looking to create the visual side: the brand identity, and the design of our site and its publication.

## Value proposition

The value proposition is that in addition to producing innovative instruments, Neotone produces knowledge of a different sort in its field. The field is the acoustic handpan, a new instrument with a rapidly growing practice and no central hub of knowledge or vocabulary. The Neotone One also extends that field into electronic music in a way the acoustic instrument cannot. This position opens different avenues into learning from both sides.

## Target audience

Our earliest adopters were professional handpan players. We have seen a growing audience of electronic musicians and DJs, interested in the instrument itself. And we would like to reach a wider audience of beginners and adult amateurs looking for an entry point into making music, whether acoustic or electronic.

## Positioning

Neotone sits between the handpan, an emerging acoustic practice; electronic music, a culture that values new and capable instruments; and the established traditions of music learning. Each has its own makers, gear and publications, and Neotone belongs fully to none of them. The tension between the three is its position. It competes on the quality of the object and the thinking around it, rather than on features and specs.

## Brand character

The following describes the direction we wish to move in, which we believe is not captured in our current site or materials.

Editorial over promotional. Ideas embedded in material. The written pieces on the wireframe are included to demonstrate the voice. The wireframe is only text at this point, but the finished site wants images of the instrument in settings that show its possibilities, and possibly video and audio.

We would also like to create a print version of our publication to include with the instrument, and to distribute independently. A site and a publication that work in the register of editorial design communicate a very different character than a typical music product site.

## Graphic elements and platforms

Our only existing graphic element is our wordmark, printed on the instrument. It is an unmodified Adobe typeface, All Round Gothic. It has worked, but we believe it can be evolved for this next phase. We have product photography, but would be interested in updating our approach to it.

The main focus is the overall brand identity and the site. Beyond that, the identity needs to carry into a brand system: tutorial videos, with their captioning and colour, packaging, and trade show design.

# Print

These examples are exploring approaches to make text feel inviting. To bring a viewer into reading.

Exaggerated text size for the full first paragraph draws the viewer directly into the text. Text becomes the focus of visual attention as though it were an image.

![](assets/5_print/point51-1.jpg)

*Point.51 Mag, Issue 01*

Typography design and text layout transform the act of reading into an experience of what the words describe.

![](assets/5_print/fukt-1.jpg)

![](assets/5_print/fukt-3.jpg)

*Fukt Magazine No. 22, Nature Issue*

Simple, clear text layout is given a splash of fun and personality by superimposing small illustrations.

*Editorial Design Reference (compost)*
